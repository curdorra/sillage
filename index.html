The Lab · formula

Compose in parts

Perfumers write formulas in parts per thousand. Start from a classic structure, change the proportions, and watch how the character shifts from spray to the next morning.

A teaching model: each material's presence is its parts × impact, fading at a rate set by its tenacity. Real evaporation also depends on vapour pressure, odour thresholds, skin and the way materials hold one another back. Real formulas use dozens of materials, many of them pre-diluted. Presets are sketches of classic structures, not the formulas of any perfume.

Kannauj, Uttar Pradesh · featured

Mitti: the first rain, bottled

In Kannauj, attar makers bake discs of clay from dried pond beds, then distil them into sandalwood oil. The result captures petrichor, the smell of rain on dry earth. Its key molecule, geosmin, is made by soil microbes, and the human nose can detect it at extraordinarily low levels.

  1. Clay discs are baked and loaded into a copper still, the deg, with water.
  2. A wood fire heats the deg; vapour travels through a bamboo pipe.
  3. It condenses into a bhapka, a receiver holding sandalwood oil, cooled in water.
  4. Over days the oil absorbs the scent. No alcohol is used: this is an attar.
